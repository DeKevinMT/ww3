# Frontier Command

Frontier Command is a real-time world-conquest game on a true political world map. Every country uses the same deterministic national AI for finance, research, recruitment, recovery and military operations. Country selection grants no hidden planning or AI-efficiency advantage, while the human commander chooses war and peace. Playing a country amplifies its own identity trait on a smooth 1×–3× curve and independently adjusts its opening Army and Army capacity on the ordinary 0.05×–15× curve, with a competitive tail for the weakest 25 starts that reaches 50× for the weakest. That opening adjustment returns gradually to 1× over the first 30 years, giving weak starts a genuine opening window while delaying the largest powers' full mobilisation. The human capital displays this phase directly on the map: teal `OPENING BOOST` and violet `OPENING LIMIT` indicators start at 100% remaining, count down with their bar and disappear at expiry. Solo play assigns one country. Public matchmaking and private Direct Connect rooms assign a different country to each player in the same world, with the same economy, research, AI and combat rules. AI skill scales only modestly with the country's bounded national-IQ gameplay score, so a higher score means slightly better execution without creating a separate ruleset.

**Standard 2026** is always the default game mode. **Alternative Universe** is an explicitly selected mode that keeps the real map and relative country sizes but deterministically regenerates population, economy, military strength, IQ and growth from the selected seed. Independent stat outliers can make a country unusually strong in one field without making every attribute move together, so alternate worlds are deliberately less predictable. The same seed produces the same world in solo play, multiplayer and saved campaigns. Rerolling creates a new seed while preserving the player's currently selected country.

The game is designed for desktop. The map contains 166 strategically useful countries; microstates and very small islands are omitted to keep the campaign readable and fast. Greenland is an explicit playable exception with its own territory, flag, economy and Arctic sea routes. The strategic map keeps the current top ten powers legible, uses sharp vector flags and supports deep zoom up to 24× so compact countries such as Luxembourg remain practical to inspect and select.

The world presentation extends beyond playable borders: subdued ocean labels restore geographic context, the ocean backdrop fills the complete viewport at every zoom, and a detailed ice-styled Antarctica sits below the ordinary political world without becoming a country. One cached screen-space Natural Earth border pass stays sharp at every zoom without a second baked outline or extra country draw calls. The North Pole is a selectable research gateway, while three Antarctic endgame corridors leave from the Drake Passage, southern Africa and Australia/New Zealand. The South American lane stays offshore instead of clipping Chile or Argentina.

## The core military model

**Manpower** is the complete deployed army and its normal combat-health pool. Every territory army stores:

- total manpower;
- its current population-, integration- and research-based capacity;
- the manpower-weighted base ATK and DEF carried by that local army.

Local ATK/DEF quality remains additive: `manpower × base quality` is conserved when armies mix. Moving, recruiting or taking casualties cannot manufacture military quality, and there is no separate battle-XP progression system.

Army capacity is intentionally simple and cannot be permanently damaged by budget trouble or a temporary crisis. Newly conquered land unlocks its population reserve gradually through its visible integration share:

```text
territory army capacity = live population × integration × 0.00145
  × (1 + Force Capacity research × 0.01)
```

Capacity automatically synchronises with live population, integration and research. Recruitment fills available national capacity; it does not purchase more capacity. Every country opens with a country-calibrated reserve: published reserve pools contribute a common 55% immediately-ready share, while missing or zero observations first receive a real 5%-of-neutral-cap cadre before any weak-start multiplier is applied. The bottom quartile then ramps toward 1.5× reserves and the weakest ten toward 2×, so no bonus ever multiplies zero; selected identities such as Belgium and Greenland may add their own bounded reserve term. Every ordinary AI result remains bounded by exactly `1 ×` live active capacity. For a human country below 1× on the opening curve, both deployed soldiers and trained reserves start lower, and both Army capacity and Reserve capacity unlock along that same 30-year return to 1×. Above 1×, the temporary Army cap is fully operational for ordinary paid recruitment, accelerated recruitment, reserve deployment and reserve training. The free opening contingent occupies real slots but never refills; only the physical gap below live capacity can be trained, and every paid replacement consumes normal food, recruitment funding and deployed upkeep. As the temporary cap returns toward 1×, further training closes automatically, while existing paid troops are never deleted merely because the cap moved. A healthy AI restores the active army to effectively 100% readiness first and then pays to train its separate reserve pool. During war, ordinary weekly recruitment no longer creates fresh active soldiers: the existing pool supplies bounded replacements and therefore depletes. Already-trained reservists mobilise at `3 ×` the matching fresh-training throughput, while paid reserve training itself continues at only 5% of its normal peacetime pace. Upkeep can rise smoothly to 125% only above the live treasury reserve target, accelerating recruitment without creating free troops. Demobilisation is forbidden in ordinary recovery and permitted only during an extreme food or debt emergency that also makes payroll genuinely unaffordable; even then at most 0.05% of deployed manpower leaves per week. A conquered frontier starts at exactly 10% of its native local cap. Every new conquest integration uses exactly `1.02 ×` the original immutable size curve: about 12.8 years for Luxembourg, 26 years for Belgium and 173 years for China. A foreign territory can station its local cap plus 10% of the empire's total Army cap when integration begins; this support allowance declines linearly to 5% at full integration. A pre-existing overshoot is never deleted: normal weekly logistics gradually moves its excess to another owned territory with room, or leaves it in place until real attrition if no destination has room. Internal land redistribution stays treasury-free. Naval redistribution pays the same distance-aware logistics logic, loses throughput with sea distance and physical country size, and may spend at most 5% of available treasury per week.

Starting ATK and DEF are calibrated separately from the capacity rule. The country's existing military power index is divided by its actually deployable opening manpower; GDP per capita and IQ supply only a small conserved 0.97×–1.03× opening-quality imprint, while SIPRI spending per deployed soldier applies an ATK-versus-DEF tilt without changing the combined 55/45 value. Each territory army keeps its own manpower-weighted base quality after that. Moving or deploying troops carries that quality, merged armies blend it by manpower, and local recruits use the starting profile of the land they come from. Annexation or federation never rewrites or weakens soldiers already deployed. Live effective ATK and DEF then apply a separate owner-wide national-systems layer: current integrated GDP per capita carries 65% and live national IQ 35%, producing 0.35×–1.65× across the opening bounds and up to 1.715× at researched IQ 112. Diminishing Economy & Science modernisation then converts at 0.75×–1.25× with IQ. The visible DEF stat, country ranking and national panels retain that complete linear number. Combat shields use 75% of its former battlefield weight; when DEF rises above the opposing ATK, only that relative advantage bends onto a smooth logarithmic diminishing-return curve. Equal ATK/DEF growth therefore preserves peer balance, every further DEF point remains useful, and extreme late-game defence can no longer scale protection linearly forever. Forecast outcomes use the combat curve while still showing the unchanged DEF stat.

Every empire has exactly one immutable country trait: the trait of its original 2026 nation. All visible trait names, effects, conditions and identity text are English. Traits are built around the real strategic identity of each country instead of compensating weak starts with generic Army-cap bonuses; the separate opening-force curve handles that balance. A human seat amplifies the signed bonuses of that same trait using a smooth 1×–3× opening-military-rank curve; fixed effects such as Arctic price affinity are explicitly excluded from that amplification. Greenland receives the full 3× endpoint in Standard 2026, but its authored mobilisation values are bounded before that multiplier so the separate opening-force system does not stack into an excessive permanent capacity bonus. The ordinary opening-force curve begins at exactly 0.05× for the United States and reaches a 15× base endpoint; a competitive tail lifts the weakest 25 starts toward 20× and the weakest ten continue smoothly to a 50× endpoint. Countries around Belgium's rank receive a smaller additional lift before that underdog tail. Deployed Army and Army capacity return gradually to 1× over 1,560 weeks. Above 1×, only the temporary deployed surplus is free: it consumes neither food nor upkeep, cannot be replenished, fades on schedule and absorbs casualties first. It does not create free trained reserves, although the live temporary cap can be filled with normal paid soldiers and reserves. Below 1×, the opening deployed Army, trained Reserve, Army capacity and Reserve capacity all begin reduced and recover together, preventing hidden full-strength reserves from bypassing the intended delayed mobilisation. Conquest, integration, revolution and federation never copy, donate or stack another country's trait.

National IQ is now an inspectable empire-fusion value instead of remaining frozen when territory changes owner. Each controlled territory contributes `live population × integration`, anchored to that population's immutable opening nation and opening IQ baseline. The result is population-weighted, then the current empire leader's national-IQ trait is applied exactly once; absorbed-country traits never stack. Diminishing Education & Intelligence research is added only after fusion. The Nation demographics card combines current integration, population-origin shares, native-to-fused IQ and research. Attack Review uses a pure full-core projection so a conquest that would lower IQ, army systems or GDP per person is visible before war starts.

## Simple economy

The Economy tab is a financial command ledger: Treasury and net cashflow lead the view, followed by matching annualised income and expense breakdowns, taxation, food trade and the live annual economy-growth rate. The current empire Treasury sits directly beside Economy in the top bar, visibly marks debt and shows the percentage filled toward the same APEX reserve target used by military overfunding. Every country starts with a visible Base Operations cost equal to 20% of ordinary tax revenue; Public Administration research can gradually lower it toward 15%, and the live percentage is shown in the ledger. The simulation still settles weekly, but player-facing recurring rates are shown per year for easier comparison. It uses one automatic country rate and one transparent tax identity:

```text
reference productive population = sum(opening population × current integration share)
live population factor = live productive population / reference productive population
fiscal wealth reference = integrated GDP / reference productive population
country tax rate = 10% + 10% × clamp(fiscal wealth reference / $75K, 0, 1)
taxable output = integrated GDP × (50% + 50% × live population factor)
weekly tax = taxable output × country tax rate ÷ 52
```

The rate therefore stays between 10% and 20%: lower-income countries sit near 10%, while financially strong countries move toward 20%. It is automatic, not a player slider. The tax rate itself has no hidden war or condition modifier; the live and reference populations of an annexed territory use the same visible integration share. At the opening baseline the population factor is exactly one, so starting income keeps its previous calibration. Afterwards tax responds monotonically to live productive population: more people raise receipts and losses lower them, while the stable 50% economy half keeps some income available after a severe demographic collapse so food recovery remains possible. Real integrated GDP, the displayed Economy value and conquest value remain separate from this fiscal blend; global rank is based only on live Combat Power. GDP grows only through the economy-growth system, reconstruction or conquest; displayed GDP per person may still fall when population grows against unchanged production.

Real economic growth is recalculated every week instead of being fixed:

```text
annual economy growth = 0.3% base
  + productive investment as a share of live GDP × 0.22
  + Economy Growth research
  + a small full-food bonus
  − food-shortage drag
  − war and post-war growth drag
```

Productive investment is capped at a normalised 12% share for the growth calculation. The result is bounded between −6% and +4.5% per year. An active war removes at least 1.2 percentage points, with extra fronts and fatigue worsening the loss; recovery remains gradual after peace. Every territory has a weighted multi-terrain profile rather than a single cosmetic label. Exactly its one to three active types are normalised to a complete 100% before the world is built, so combat, logistics, economics, the map and every UI surface use the same weights. Coastal remains one of those three for countries with an ocean coast, while landlocked countries can never receive it. The complete mix affects defender strength, operational supply, war cost, condition repair, food production and economic growth, so desert, mountain, jungle, Arctic, urban, plains and coastal land carry distinct logistical as well as military and economic trade-offs. The map communicates that complete mix through terrain colour and border cues, while tooltips and the selected-country panel show the same active types, deterministic whole-number shares totalling exactly 100%, and their combined effects. Domestic food is bought first; imports cover the remaining demand at a higher price. `100% incoming` means this week's needs are covered; production or imports above 100% become stored surplus. Food may fall below the safe balance: increasingly deep shortages first suppress growth and then cause slow but visible famine deaths. Food has a real national storage limit measured against live weekly demand: a healthy country targets 12 weeks, physical capacity stays between 8 and 36 weeks, and even a deliberately fragile opening receives a five-week emergency floor. Controlled landmass, wealth, traits and Food Systems research still create meaningful storage depth inside that range. The top bar shows `current / maximum` food and the annualized storage gain or loss. The Economy drawer subtracts imports from exports and explicitly labels the result as **Net Food Imports**, **Net Food Exports** or balanced trade, so a country that imports food is never described as merely having no exports. This makes reserves a real multi-month buffer instead of a short countdown, while fragile countries still carry a much larger food cost and begin below the healthy target. A rich, poor, populous, agricultural or food-insecure conquest changes the combined economy and food system in a different, visible way.

Food purchasing is preventative as well as reactive. The funded request rises smoothly as stock falls below its country-specific target and progressively draws on positive treasury reserves instead of waiting for weekly coverage to fail. During a live shortage, extra starvation mortality begins below 10% of the safe target stock and increases toward the empty-reserve maximum.

For steadier long campaigns, each World Bank population-growth starting value is moved exactly halfway toward 1% annual growth:

```text
balanced growth = 1% + 0.5 × (source growth − 1%)
```

This raises the lowest figures, lowers the highest figures and preserves the complete country ordering. The adjustment happens once when game content is built and adds no work to weekly simulation.

Every national AI manages one treasury and three priorities:

- **Armed Forces** pays army upkeep, wartime operations and recruitment.
- **Research** funds the ten Development programs.
- **National Economy** supports repair, economic growth and population growth.

Food remains the first required weekly cost. When food reserves fall below one week or food security becomes critical, the country may spend its positive cash reserve to close the emergency food gap. In peace, food shortage, population decline, exhausted reserves or debt place every AI in survival recovery: development and essential research take priority and optional wars stop. Low post-war condition or an ordinary deficit never authorises demobilisation; a solvent, fed country protects payroll and rebuilds toward a full army. Only an extreme combined survival and affordability crisis permits the 0.05%-per-week drawdown, and at least 25% of live Army capacity remains as a home guard. During war, every viable source-unique owned army opens and pays for its own real front, so a multi-country empire can attack simultaneously from several territories. A campaign continues across the enemy's remaining territories until peace, withdrawal or capitulation. Treasury may fall below zero: new borrowing costs 10%, the first revenue-week of debt remains a recoverable grace band, and deeper debt progressively raises import costs and contracts recurring-program funding. Existing debt begins a small carrying premium after two revenue-weeks, reaches full activation after eight, and is capped at 25% of weekly revenue; pressure reaches its severe and critical stages at 26 and 52 revenue-weeks respectively.

The shared treasury policy deliberately builds a liquid emergency buffer: roughly eight weeks of tax revenue in peace, or six weeks plus two per active war, with only small IQ and economy-size adjustments. While building it, the planner retains a bounded share of otherwise free cash; even a funded reserve keeps 5% of free cashflow. For AI countries and the human country's APEX planner alike, cash above 10% of live controlled GDP is gradually reinvested through existing readiness, research and Development systems. The draw ramps in over the next 5% of GDP and is capped each week at 2% of the true excess and 25% of weekly revenue, so a windfall remains stable rather than disappearing at once. Autonomous countries do not drain that buffer through one-off Rapid Recruitment, Research Surge or Propaganda purchases. The common planner reviews recurring policy every eight weeks and moves budget and research allocations toward their targets by only two to four percentage points in total per review, with national IQ determining the small bounded step. This keeps allocation-driven costs and priorities gradual; real events such as war, conquest or lost revenue can still change mandatory costs directly. Before Antarctic first contact, the human country's Suspicion raises APEX Armed Forces priority on a convex curve: a highly exposed player deliberately preserves readiness, reserves and military funding instead of continuing a peacetime allocation. Once the rogue AI is revealed, every surviving country switches to a fixed Earth Defence military priority regardless of Suspicion. None of these rules grants free units or bypasses treasury, recruitment or upkeep.

## Research

All ten programs progress automatically at the same time. The national AI gradually redistributes attention according to each country's current needs:

- **Population & Recruitment** — population growth or training speed.
- **Military Industry** — force capacity or cheaper recruitment.
- **Advanced Weapons** — ATK or reinforcement efficiency.
- **Defensive Systems** — DEF or casualty reduction.
- **Logistics & Medicine** — replenishment or supply.
- **Economy & Science** — economic growth, research speed or research efficiency.
- **Food Systems** — domestic food output or strategic storage.
- **Reserve Doctrine** — reserve training or wartime mobilisation.
- **Public Administration** — tax collection or lower base operations.
- **Education & Intelligence** — a very expensive, diminishing live-IQ increase capped at 112.

Every breakthrough adds a deterministic seeded +1% result from its branch. Requirements grow along the `(B + 1) × 1.18^B` mastery curve, so research can continue indefinitely while later improvements take longer. Research allocations use the same eight-week, two-to-four-point transition limit as the national budget and therefore never jump straight to a newly calculated target.

## Arctic research and Antarctic endgame

Clicking the North Pole—or using the gateway inside Research—opens a separate manual black-research programme. Its four projects are deliberately slow, expensive and sequential. Their full cost is paid up front while ordinary national research continues in parallel:

1. **Polar Habitat Genome** improves population growth, recovery and food storage.
2. **Cryogenic Logistics Grid** improves supply, casualty reduction and research efficiency.
3. **Strategic Mobilisation Vaults** improves Army capacity, reserve training and reserve mobilisation.
4. **Deep-Ice Signal Array** improves attack, defence and research speed, then exposes an impossible transmission beneath Antarctica.

Only the completed phases and the next available phase are shown. Base research windows are 156, 260, 416 and 780 weeks. Owning one of the eight Arctic gateways grants access; every additional gateway shortens the window by 5 percentage points, up to 35%, after which the existing Research Speed effect is applied. A project's final window and upfront payment are frozen when it is authorised.

Base costs are $80B, $180B, $385B and $760B. Price is influenced only by the immutable starter-country military-rank factor and Arctic affinity; current empire economy never changes it. The rank factor runs smoothly from 0.50× for the weakest original start to 10.00× for the strongest. Greenland, Iceland, Norway, Canada, Finland, Sweden and Russia lower Arctic costs by 12.5%, 35%, 30%, 25%, 22%, 18% and 12% respectively; the United States pays a 15% Arctic penalty. Conquest never changes this national identity.

Completing the signal array creates a persistent warning that must be acknowledged before the campaign continues. Only the three coastal gateway sectors are visible at first. Deploying trained reserves through any gateway reveals the hidden machine force and starts the Earth Defence phase. Ordinary interstate wars and offers end immediately; every surviving country joins the global military mobilisation regardless of Suspicion, while the defence coalition coordinates the counteroffensive, shares lagging research and stops opening new wars against humanity. Suspicion remains strategically harmful because it slows trust and coalition strength, but damage inflicted on the rogue intelligence now earns permanent Suspicion relief.

The Antarctic campaign is a fixed nine-sector directed network: three entry sectors, three outer installations, two inner strongholds and the **Zero Point Core**. Each expedition is paid from real trained reserves, advances on a deterministic four-week battle pulse and returns surviving personnel subject to the live reserve cap. Enemy strength escalates by depth and global wave. The core has three boss phases; destroying its final phase is the actual campaign victory, so ordinary world conquest no longer ends a human game before the polar threat is resolved. See [the endgame design and tuning notes](docs/POLAR_ENDGAME.md).

## War and conquest

Land and naval access use different physical routes. A land attack requires a direct shared border. Any two coastal countries can use a valid direct naval lane: it starts on each country's principal landmass, follows the same curved path in simulation and on the map, and is rejected only when that path cuts through a third country's land. Distance has no reach cap; it raises operating cost and supply pressure using the complete sailed distance rather than a centre-to-centre shortcut. The compact Attack Review keeps the win chance, direct attack-versus-defence matchups, added weekly and annual cost, total recurring operations, cashflow after start and route premium. Conquest Value uses retained post-war economy and population, shows their percentage growth against the current empire, and previews full-core IQ, army-quality and GDP-per-person fusion before commitment. Best-target recommendations keep forecast strength dominant while penalising distance and giving bounded value preference to high GDP per capita and IQ.

Wars are persistent operations resolved on a deterministic combat cadence. Every viable, source-unique army route becomes a real simultaneous front; the complete deployed source and target armies on those fronts contribute to pressure, take losses, appear on the map and add operations cost. ATK, DEF, terrain, supply, condition and supporting armies all matter. There is no hidden universal defender multiplier: the former 1.25× position layer is exactly 1×, so visible terrain and logistics now provide the positional advantage. Naval fronts extend to long-haul global lanes. Route load is `1.35 + 0.80 × smoothstep(1,500, 9,000, km) + 0.55 × max(0, km − 5,000) / 5,000`: every route beyond 5,000 km therefore continues becoming more expensive instead of reaching a hidden plateau. Supply starts near 0.98× and approaches its bounded long-route floor near 0.90×. Naval access has no separate assault or casualty penalty. Physical country size adds a separate smooth internal-operations load of at most 25%, so Russia cannot move and sustain forces as if it were Luxembourg. Effective damage uses a `0.008` (0.8%) multiplier against the opposing pressure/shield ratio. Individual battle pulses roll independently from 0.80× to 1.30× around a 1.05× opening mean. Over the first 52 combat weeks that range shifts linearly to 0.90×–1.40× around a 1.15× mean, so opening attacks remain smaller while long wars become progressively more lethal without producing extreme damage spikes. The two seeded rolls remain deterministic for saves and multiplayer. Requested losses are bounded only by remaining local manpower. Every accepted declaration immediately removes exactly 1% of the attacker's deployed army before the first battle and records it in the ordinary cumulative military losses. When an attacker has both local and national overmatch above 3:1, defender counter-fire exposure rises smoothly, capped at 6× by 8:1; peer wars remain unchanged.

War Pressure rates sustainability from live wars, fronts, duration, fatigue and military readiness. A fresh single land war starts low, prolonged campaigns now build more gradually, and simultaneous wars remain the main operational penalty; extra fronts inside one conflict have diminishing weight and naval routes count as only 0.25 of a land front. Conquest creates no separate War Pressure aftershock; only its ordinary fatigue contributes. Peacetime fatigue still clears by 0.33 points per week before country-trait modifiers. Suspicion retains its deliberately slower independent recovery.

AI political reactions use a separate **Expansion Threat** signal rather than general War Pressure. One long or punishing war—especially by a small country—remains a low expansion threat by itself. Several simultaneous offensive wars and the conquest of multiple unique sovereign countries inside a rolling 104-week window raise it sharply; land and population gained only amplify the signal after one of those expansion patterns exists. Expansion Threat is the fast internal signal, while **Suspicion** is the slow 0–100 reputation meter that ultimately scales every autonomous AI declaration against a human. At exactly 0 Suspicion that chance is exactly 0%; very low values remain exceptionally safe, while the convex curve makes high values progressively dangerous. Every positive Suspicion source now receives the same modest 1.15× accumulation factor, so a single war remains manageable while clustered wars, rapid conquest and sudden power growth become noticeably riskier. Peaceful recovery is unchanged at about 1.3 points per year. An AI supporter evaluates its own force together with the country already defending against the aggressor, but uses a bounded, seed-stable intelligence error and a probability roll. It can therefore intervene earlier against an expansionist great power without turning every assessment into an automatic player loss. A defensive intervention opens a separate bilateral war against the aggressor only: the supporter never becomes hostile to the country it is helping. Human players can use the same structure to rescue a defender.

War Score now changes after real battle pulses from strength-relative casualty exchange, momentum and territorial conquest instead of remaining at zero. Its scale-independent casualty component keeps equally efficient small and large battles comparable, while a conquest adds a clear strategic swing. When several wars compete for the same source army, battle scheduling gives the longest-neglected eligible conflict priority; one fixed war ID can no longer monopolise the force and push the others into artificial stalemates.

Army economics also follows live GDP per capita through a bounded square-root cost-of-living curve. Low-income mass armies pay materially less for soldier upkeep, recruitment, reserve training and the manpower portion of war operations; wealthy high-quality forces pay more per soldier. The 0.58×–1.45× bounds keep North Korea-style force structures financially possible without making poverty an unlimited free-army exploit. GDP-scaled war administration, weapons quality, real defence spending, army size and country traits continue to apply separately.

A country with a negative treasury cannot declare a new war. Existing wars continue normally, and the central declaration rule unlocks again as soon as treasury reaches zero or higher, so neither human commands nor AI paths can bypass debt recovery. Human countries have no fixed calendar shield: Suspicion fully replaces the former two-year protection and can expose an aggressive player immediately, while exact zero remains perfectly safe from autonomous AI declarations. An AI aggressor at or below 10% of its live Army capacity stops opening offensive battles, asks for peace immediately and pays 1.5× the ordinary ceasefire price. Defender counterattacks remain active while that withdrawal is pending.

A voluntary paid ceasefire distributes its fixed treaty cost across 52 weekly instalments. When the final payment is made, both signatories remain protected from restarting that war for another 104 weeks. Player offers are credible often enough to serve as a real exit route, especially against a fatigued, depleted or multi-front AI, but a revenge claim or an opponent close to conquest still makes acceptance difficult. Longer AI campaigns can also end through genuine collapse, exhaustion or prolonged stalemate instead of remaining open indefinitely.

Battle damage still causes bounded local civilian deaths on both sides. Those losses remain in the affected territories: there is no cross-border migration, refugee or displacement subsystem.

Conquest preserves the surviving population, economy and infrastructure as the territory's full long-term potential. Territory stays completely with its defender throughout combat and transfers directly, in full, only after decisive conquest; there is no partial-control state. A depleted formation may surrender only after at least 26 weeks on the same strongly dominant front, at no more than 12.5% local readiness, after losses equal to at least 80% of local capacity and while outnumbered at least four to one. Surrendered remnants leave active manpower but are not reported as battle deaths. A foreign owner receives exactly 10% of its population capacity, taxable output, food production and army capacity immediately. The remaining 90% unlocks smoothly on the immutable `1.02 ×` original population/GDP/land-area calendar: Luxembourg takes about 12.8 years, Belgium 26 and China 173. The base administration quote is 3% per year of the territory's live GDP at capture; the active owner's one trait may adjust that quote, after which it is frozen and paid only while the program exists. A human player's first conquest integration costs only 25% of that final quote—a one-time 75% reduction tracked separately for every multiplayer human. Completion, sovereign recapture or revolution ends it.

Every conquest integration retains its seed-stable ordinary 2% revolution chance, scheduled between 20% and 80% of its calendar. At War Pressure 75 or higher, still-integrating conquest territory gains a separate bounded pressure risk that rises from 10% toward 35%; peaceful federation integrations never receive that overreach bonus. A revolution restores the territory and surviving live stats to its immutable 2026 start owner—even if that nation had been absorbed—replaces the occupation force with a bounded local rebel army and immediately begins a normal independence war against the occupier. That war includes the same visible 1% attacker opening loss as every other declaration. At completion without revolution, `coreOwner` becomes the current owner and the program is deleted. The former country name and flag disappear, but player-owned core territory retains a subtle compact local-power bar and exact number; an actively integrating territory keeps its compact progress and local-defence value. Both integration and ordinary borders use the same sharp screen-space geometry. A real conquest guard is drawn from the surviving attacking source: up to 10% of that army moves across, bounded by the territory's local cap plus 10% of total empire Army cap at the start; this scalable support share declines linearly to 5% at full integration. It never creates soldiers or national cap.

AI finance reserves every frozen integration bill before it calculates food and discretionary military, research or development envelopes, so the same weekly revenue cannot be promised twice. Integration costs equal to at least 15% of revenue progressively increase the required war chest; at 40% they block optional AI expansion until the burden or fiscal position improves.

Peaceful defensive federation is an accelerated version of the same visible core-fusion process, not an instant stat rewrite. Ownership changes when the union begins, each joining territory integrates over `0.25 ×` its conquest duration, and its live population, economy, condition and deployed army quality remain unchanged. The member's treasury, food, trained reserves and strongest research remain conserved until its final core completes, then transfer exactly once before the retired backend identity is removed.

Rival expansion is deliberately restrained:

- normal AI war starts have a 78-week global cooldown;
- regional escalations have a 104-week cooldown;
- defensive interventions have a 52-week cooldown;
- the global active-war cap grows only from 2 to 4;
- an ordinary country may have at most one active war;
- only a major power after week 260 may sustain two;
- normal expansion targets peaceful ordinary countries, avoiding states already caught in another war.
- each eight-week AI review gets only one ordinary expansion commitment roll; normal chances remain modest and opportunistic dogpiles are sharply suppressed.
- a human empire with a real multi-war or rapid-conquest expansion pattern enters an additional political-response review lane; Expansion Threat creates urgency, but Suspicion remains the final human-target chance gate and neither signal bypasses routes, truces, finance, food or forecasts.

Expansion-driven suspicion can still create permanent defensive federations, but coalition recruitment cannot begin before year three, adds at most one member per year and needs five aligned members before loose containment activates. Federation checks occur only every two years, require threat 75–86, and successful waves remain six to eight years apart; reactions to a rapidly expanding AI cannot begin before year 16 and use the same two-year windows. Each new union starts with two countries and gains at most one member per later wave. Its founding pair permanently produces a deterministic name ending in **Defense Federation**. These systems do not bypass treasury, access, combat or global war limits and grant no ATK, DEF, forecast, declaration or other hidden multiplier.

## Controls

- Choose a country from the single pure military-ranking picker; its score is current Combat Power only. The same order is used by the picker, top bar and ranking display. Aggressiveness is not exposed as a country-selection stat. Every visible opening stat is coloured from red through orange to green by its neutral percentile against the complete roster, explicitly excluding player start and human-trait bonuses. The list can still sort by GDP per person and the other visible neutral metrics. A separate fullscreen recommendation appears over the picker, explains Esc/F11 and allows either fullscreen or windowed play; country selection then keeps a minimum two-second loading window before map synchronisation.
- The campaign advances automatically at one week per real second.
- Click a country for a compact intelligence card centred on Combat Power, ownership, economy, population, local force and terrain. The attack action remains near the top instead of being buried below the scroll. Attack Review is decision-first: win chance, direct attack-versus-defence matchups, route, recurring cost, post-start cashflow, losses and the retained conquest value remain; duplicated warnings and explanatory tiles are removed. The disabled start button itself carries the concrete reason when a declaration is unavailable.
- Scroll to zoom as deep as 24×, drag to pan and press `Esc` to close the current panel. The top ten realms and active countries—human control, selection, war, live fronts, integration or opening mobilisation—keep compact strategic labels without growing at distance; all other countries remain collision-limited. Integrating labels show local power and progress, while completed player cores retain only a subtle local-power bar and number. A single cached screen-space line system renders ordinary and integration borders sharply at close zoom and lets the thin detail recede naturally when zoomed out; there is no second baked outline. Stronger terrain tinting on land and border cues makes physical geography visible without hiding ownership. Peaceful country statistics refresh on a staggered monthly cadence, while active wars update live. Land warfare uses a warm solid contact line and naval warfare a cool curved dashed route. Principal-landmass anchors prevent tiny islands from becoming ocean springboards, but valid Pacific and other long-haul crossings remain possible at their real cost. Clicking either pole moves the camera to its authored overview and opens the matching Arctic or Antarctic command surface; revealed Antarctic sectors can then be focused individually.
- War, Nation, Research and Economy now share one command-interface style with the top bar, country inspector, attack review, War Command and War Pressure overlays. **War** is a true military overview built around National Strength, deployed forces, reserves, capacity, quality and active campaign controls instead of duplicating the map's war card; its bounded Antarctic theatre sits at the bottom. **Nation** combines population, territory, food, condition, integration and IQ fusion in one demographics flow, with National Identity placed at the bottom. **Research** removes repeated summary material, gives active effects a stronger visual hierarchy and keeps the ten programs easy to scan. **Economy** remains purely financial, with its income ledger directly above the matching expense breakdown and **Treasury** as the leading balance. Every top-bar card has concise hover guidance, while the sound button keeps music and effects controls available without consuming a metric slot. Weekly rerenders preserve drawer scroll anchors, so long panels remain smooth and do not jump back to the top.
- War Pressure shows fatigue, fronts, peacetime recovery and the number of still-integrating territories exposed to revolution. A prominent Suspicion bar is the single visible AI-attack-risk meter, with readable safety and danger bands; brief Expansion Threat context explains why recent multi-war or rapid-conquest behaviour is accelerating it, without a second neighbour-counterattack gauge. War Command presents each campaign's live War Score, relative losses, route and peace options in the same visual system. Reports appear one at a time. Solo play pauses until the queue is continued; in multiplayer each report blocks only that local interface while the shared host clock keeps running. Conquest gains still travel subtly from the captured country to matching top-bar metrics, and bottom notifications remain readable for 3.2 seconds normally, 4 seconds for war events and 5 seconds for conquests, with at most four visible at once.

## Multiplayer

Public matchmaking lets a player enter one live queue without choosing a lobby size or exchanging codes. Compatible players are placed in an open lobby, and more commanders may join until the elected host starts. Every connected player chooses a unique country and marks ready. Private Direct Connect rooms remain available for friends who prefer manual invites.

The production queue runs at `wss://frontier-command-matchmaking.dekevinmt.workers.dev/matchmaking`. The client ships with that endpoint as its secure default; `VITE_MATCHMAKING_URL` remains available for explicit local or staging overrides.

The host is authoritative: its tab owns the shared clock, validates that every command belongs to the sender's country, assigns the global action order and broadcasts deterministic ticks. Guests run synchronized replicas, compare a canonical checkpoint every eight eligible ticks and request an authoritative snapshot if they diverge. Only the host changes shared speed. Each local interface uses its own assigned-country viewer, locally read inbox state and war-report queue; those presentation choices never change the shared save or hash.

Campaign connections use browser WebRTC data channels with a public **STUN-only** configuration. Public matchmaking automates discovery and signaling, while private rooms expose the manual invite/answer exchange. The host tab must remain open for the entire game. There is no TURN relay fallback, so restrictive school, office, carrier-grade or mobile networks may block a direct peer route.

## Architecture and verification

The deterministic TypeScript simulation, AI, persistence, multiplayer protocol and map content remain isolated from Phaser rendering and the DOM HUD. Canonical saves use **schema 22** and rules version **`frontier-command-v2.66-strategic-rebalance`**. Schema 22 stores the sorted `humanPlayerIds` roster, alliances, war-revenge state and bounded polar campaign state; the local viewer and polar camera focus remain runtime-only. Authenticated v2.65 same-schema saves preserve their complete payload while upgrading to v2.66; authenticated v2.64 pre-polar saves are verified against their original payload before the initial polar state is injected and re-signed. Authenticated schema 13–21 migrations remain deterministic, and existing integration endpoints remain unchanged.

The current rules keep those deterministic performance boundaries while adding Standard/Alternative Universe mode selection, 30-year human opening-force curves, multi-terrain economics and logistics, first-conquest relief, pressure revolutions, bilateral defensive interventions, live War Score, exhaustion peace, expansion-pattern counterattacks, fair shared-source multi-war scheduling and the bounded polar endgame. Development and tests still run exhaustive invariants every tick; production runs the same full scan every eight ticks and forces it immediately on terminal paths. Polar simulation is constant-size and uses independent hash-derived battle rolls so it cannot shift the ordinary world RNG sequence. Rendering separately caches peaceful map statistics for four-week staggered refreshes, uses one static border-detail draw call and one instanced Antarctic marker draw call, and keeps the idle globe at its low frame rate.
